# 3 · Legal and compliance checklist (Switzerland first, then Germany and Austria)

> I'm not a lawyer. This is a map of what to check. Confirm the electrical safety part with the ESTI (Swiss electrical inspectorate) or an electrician, and the tax part with a fiduciary (Treuhänder).

## Before the first sale (Switzerland)

- [ ] **Register the business.** A sole proprietorship (Einzelfirma) is enough. Commercial-register entry becomes mandatory at CHF 100k revenue but is useful earlier (trust, bank account, the Galaxus marketplace).
- [ ] **AHV/AVS (social security) as self-employed.** Register with your cantonal compensation office.
- [ ] **VAT.** Mandatory from CHF 100k worldwide revenue a year. Below that, register voluntarily only if you'll mostly sell B2B.
- [ ] **Lamp safety (Swiss low-voltage ordinance NEV, SR 734.26).** As the maker, you must:
  - meet the safety goals of the EU low-voltage directive (Annex I)
  - keep a **declaration of conformity** for 10 years, in German, French, Italian or English, with your name and address ([ESTI FAQ](https://www.esti.admin.ch/de/themen/faq/elektrische-erzeugnisse-marktueberwachung), [NEV](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/2016/17/20230101/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-2016-17-20230101-de-pdf-a.pdf))
  - keep a **technical file**: parts list with each part's certificate, wiring diagram, temperature test log, strain-relief pull test, label photos
  - follow up on your products in the market and report faults
  - Self-declaration is legally enough. An ESTI safety mark is voluntary.
- [ ] **Relevant standards** to name in the declaration: EN 60598-1 and EN 60598-2-1 (fixed lamps) or 2-4 (portable), EN 55015 / EN 61547 (EMC), EN 62493.
  - Easiest route: the cord set, socket and LED kit are already certified, and you only do the mechanical assembly. Your own tests are then strain relief, temperature and stability.
- [ ] **Label on each lamp:** maker name and address, type/model, rated voltage, max. bulb wattage ("max. 7 W LED"), Class II symbol, CE mark (if also sold in the EU), crossed-out bin symbol.
- [ ] **E-waste: the advance recycling fee (vRG) for lamps.** Lamps fall under the Swiss e-waste ordinance (VREG). Register with **SENS / Stiftung Licht Recycling Schweiz (SLRS)** and pay the fee per lamp sold ([SENS/SLRS](https://www.swico.ch/media/filer_public/fe/6b/fe6b9703-447c-4938-b039-a20c29bfe21a/fachbericht_swico_sens_slrs_2014_1.pdf)). The amount is small, about CHF 0.x per lamp; confirm with SENS.
- [ ] **Business liability insurance with product liability.** Get quotes from AXA, Mobiliar and Helvetia; cover of CHF 3–5 million.
- [ ] **Website legal pages:** imprint (Impressum), privacy policy under the Swiss data protection act (revDSG; if you sell to the EU, the GDPR too), terms (AGB). Swiss law gives online buyers no statutory right of withdrawal. A voluntary **30-day return** policy is still worth offering for trust.
- [ ] **Licensing:** you design yourself, which is fine. Never sell prints of Printables/Thingiverse models without a commercial licence.
- [ ] **"Made in Switzerland" / "Swiss Made":** allowed for industrial products if at least 60% of the manufacturing cost arises in CH and the essential step happens there. Printing and assembly in CH should qualify, but do the maths, because imported electrics count against you.

## Before selling to Germany and Austria (the EU)

- [ ] **CE mark and EU declaration of conformity** (same content as the Swiss one).
- [ ] **GPSR (EU product safety rules) responsible person in the EU.** Because you're outside the EU, you **must** name a responsible person established in the EU (an authorised representative or a fulfilment provider). Their name and address go on the product or packaging and **on every product page** ([GPSR summary](https://www.cc.lu/fileadmin/user_upload/tx_ccnews/PRODUCT_SAFETY_GPSR.pdf)). Service providers charge about EUR 300–1,000 a year.
- [ ] **WEEE (EU e-waste) registration** in Germany (stiftung ear) and Austria. In Germany non-EU sellers need an authorised representative. This is the biggest hassle.
- [ ] **Packaging registration (LUCID)** in Germany for packaging that reaches German consumers.
- [ ] **VAT and customs:** goods from CH to the EU are exports. Either the buyer pays import VAT and handling (bad experience), or you use IOSS for orders up to EUR 150 or a fulfilment partner in Germany. Talk to a fiduciary before you switch it on.
- [ ] **14-day right of withdrawal** for EU consumers, with a sample withdrawal form.

**Verdict:** launch in **Switzerland only (plus Liechtenstein)**. Take Germany and Austria in year 2 through a German fulfilment service that also acts as your GPSR and WEEE representative. Meanwhile, the English and German pages already rank in all three countries, so you can collect a waitlist ("Notify me when you ship to Germany").
