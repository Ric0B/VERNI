Are 3D printed lamps safe? What to know about heat, PLA and electrics

PLA softens at 55 °C. Why printed lamps can still be safe, what to check before you buy, and which bulb to use.

The problem is heat

PLA, the most common 3D printing plastic, softens from about 55 to 60 °C. An old 60-watt incandescent bulb reaches well over 100 °C at its surface. In a tight, closed shade the material would deform. That is why a printed lamp should never take an incandescent or halogen bulb.

LED makes the difference

A 5-watt LED bulb gives as much light as a 40-watt incandescent but produces a fraction of the heat. Even so, the air in a closed shade can reach 50 °C. Good design therefore takes care of three things: distance between bulb and shade, openings for air, and heat-resistant material such as PETG right at the socket.

What to check before you buy

  • Does the lamp state a maximum wattage, for example "max. 7 W LED"?
  • Are the socket, cable and plug certified parts with CE or ENEC marks?
  • Is the cable secured inside with strain relief, so it cannot be pulled out of the socket?
  • Does the maker give their name and address? In Switzerland and the EU this is required.

How we do it

Every new shape runs for four hours with the strongest LED we allow. We measure the shade wall with an infrared thermometer and only release a shape when it stays well below the softening point. The readings are part of each lamp's technical file.
